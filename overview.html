Checklist

Ask for before an offer

  • Floor plan with areas, energy certificate
  • Flats: declaration of division (Teilungserklärung), owners' meeting minutes for 3 years, budget (Wirtschaftsplan), last service-charge statement, repair-reserve balance, planned levies
  • Land register extract (Grundbuch), easements and building charges (Baulasten), contamination register
  • If let: tenancy agreement, rent, notice situation, date of the condominium split
  • Heating: type, age, maintenance records

At the viewing

  • Damp: basement, window reveals, smell; roof and gutters
  • Noise at rush hour; trains; flight path
  • Sun and light at different times; neighbours
  • Bathrooms and guest set-up for long family visits
  • Parking, bike storage, accessibility for older visitors

Money and rules

  • NRW: transfer tax 6.5%, notary + land registry ~2%, broker usually 3.57% (buyer half)
  • Rates jump above 60 / 70 / 80% loan-to-value
  • After buying an old house: some heating/insulation duties within 2 years (GEG)
  • Funding: KfW 261 (renovation to efficiency-house standard), KfW 458 (heating), BAFA/tax credit for single measures – check current terms
  • Buying a let flat to live in: notice periods 3–9 months, hardship objections, possible block after a condo split
  • A building surveyor (~€1,000–1,500) pays for itself in negotiation

Method and assumptions

  • Model: monthly simulation over 25 years. Every option gets the same monthly budget and the same starting capital; housing costs come out of the budget and the rest is invested in a world index fund. Personal figures are kept private; only differences are shown here.
  • Reference: the 2012 maisonette in Ippendorf at a model price of €600k. “vs reference” = wealth after 20 years minus the reference's.
  • Mortgage: 3% repayment; 10-year fixed rate by loan-to-value (≤60% 4.05%, ≤70% 4.15%, ≤80% 4.25%, ≤90% 4.45%), refinanced at 4.25% afterwards.
  • Markets: prices and rents +3%/yr, inflation 2.3%, index fund 6.5%/yr nominal taxed at 26.375% with 30% partial exemption; 4% selling costs at the end. Scenario range: fund 5 / 6.5 / 8% × prices 2 / 3 / 4%.
  • Per home: rent of a comparable home, running costs, upkeep and renovation budgets are estimates from the listings and Bonn market data. Renovation projects assume a KfW 261 tranche (~€150k at ~2.9%, 10% grant).
  • Quality scores (0–5): see What counts. Home criteria are judged per listing with a written reason; neighbourhood criteria come from OpenStreetMap, measured from the district centre; space is computed from m².
  • Not captured: light, layout, neighbours, and how much extra space is worth to you – that is what the weights are for.
